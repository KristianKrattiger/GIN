# GIN 01 — Foundations

> The reframe, the philosophical ground, and the binding to [[CALICHE_INDEX|Caliche]]. This document establishes what GIN is *for* before any mechanism explains how it works.

---

## The reframe

Earlier versions positioned GIN against the centralised internet. That framing was a strategic error. It invited a fight GIN cannot win and does not need to win, and it obscured the actual contribution.

GIN is not a replacement for the centralised internet, nor for centralised AI. It is **public-interest knowledge infrastructure**: the AI-era member of the same family as the public library, the public archive, and public broadcasting. Each of those institutions is (a) genuinely valuable, (b) not a replacement for the commercial sector, (c) funded by public-interest mechanisms rather than purely market ones, and (d) governed locally rather than homogenised nationally.

The public library did not have to defeat the bookstore. Public broadcasting did not have to defeat commercial television. They occupy a different niche, serving different values, and that is enough. **GIN does not have to defeat the commercial AI layer to be worth building.** It has to be a thing libraries, archives, universities, and sovereign communities want to operate because it serves their mission better than the alternatives, and a thing people use because it gives them something the commercial layer cannot.

---

## What the commercial layer cannot provide

Two structural gaps justify a public-interest layer.

**Interface centralisation.** A handful of general-purpose models have become the default interface to knowledge for hundreds of millions of people. The training-data choices, preference tuning, and refusal patterns of those models become de facto epistemology at scale. GIN offers an alternative interface whose epistemic commitments are explicit, governed, and auditable rather than emergent and proprietary.

**Convergence pressure.** Synthesis at scale, as the commercial layer performs it, produces convergence: heterogeneous knowledge gets averaged toward the dominant framework, and the averaging is invisible. GIN's contribution is to make the politics of knowledge production legible — to surface divergence where it is legitimate (see [[GIN_02_Productive_Divergence]]) and to concentrate verified knowledge where the question is empirical, with full provenance in both cases.

---

## The reframe extended: from infrastructure to institution

v0.4 acknowledges what v0.3 implied but did not state. Once GIN holds a validity layer that decides which differences count, and a classification function that decides what is empirical versus situated, it is exercising epistemic power. The architecture was designed to *distribute and make legible* epistemic power. The act of governing it concentrates a specific kind of power by necessity.

This is not a contradiction to hide. It is the defining tension, and v0.4 puts it at the centre. The answer is not to pretend the power does not exist but to hold it in an institution whose non-neutrality is legible and accountable — the GIN Epistemic Council ([[GIN_10_Epistemic_Council]]). The library analogy holds here too: a library exercises power through acquisition and cataloguing choices. The defensible library is not the one that pretends to neutrality but the one whose choices are visible, contestable, and governed.

## Caliche binding

GIN inherits its values from [[CALICHE_INDEX|Caliche]] rather than re-arguing them. The remnant principle — *we use remnants to create something new* — grounds [[GIN_04_TRAC|TRAC]]'s extractive synthesis. The inheritance-and-acknowledgement ethic grounds the attribution requirement. The commitment to making seams visible rather than hiding them grounds transparent balkanization (see [[GIN_07_Governance_Validity]]). The values are not invented for GIN; they are Caliche expressed as infrastructure.
